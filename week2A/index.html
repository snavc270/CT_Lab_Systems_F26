All About Sharks

Sharks have been swimming in Earth's oceans for over 400 million years — longer than trees have existed on land. There are more than 500 known species, ranging from the size of a human hand to longer than a school bus.

What makes sharks unique?

Unlike most fish, a shark's skeleton is made entirely of cartilage instead of bone, which makes them lighter and more flexible in the water.

Sharks also have an incredible sense of smell — some species can detect a single drop of blood in an amount of water equivalent to a small swimming pool.

Types of sharks

  • Great white shark
    • Can grow up to 20 feet long
    • Known for powerful, fast swimming
  • Hammerhead shark
  • Whale shark (the largest fish in the world)

How a shark hunts

  1. Detects prey using smell, sound, or electrical signals
  2. Approaches quietly, often from below
  3. Uses a burst of speed to close the distance
  4. Bites down using rows of replaceable teeth

A quote worth remembering

"Sharks are one of the ocean's oldest success stories — survivors, not monsters."
